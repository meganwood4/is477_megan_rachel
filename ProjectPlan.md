# Overview
### Our overarching goal: To explore how urban planning is associated with and affects crime patterns in cities.
We aim to understand how both features of urban infrastructure and housing related demographics are related to different types of crime. We intend to identify measurable relationships between variables such as access to public transportation, access to street lighting, housing composition, and percentage of affordable housing units in an area and how these factors relate to the amount and types of crime. By studying these patterns, we hope to uncover data-driven insights that encourage safer, more equitable city design. Urban planners and policymakers often make decisions that influence public safety indirectly through zoning, lighting infrastructure, and access to public amenities, yet these decisions are not always made with the story real data tells in mind. Our project aims to address this oversight by applying data science techniques to evaluate how specific characteristics of urban infrastructure are linked to where and when crimes happen.

We chose Chicago as our primary research location due to its diverse neighborhoods, complex history of urban development and socioeconomic disparities, and extensive data resources. However, our analysis could be replicated for other large cities in the future using similar datasets. Insights from this study can later serve as a model for understanding similar relationships in other urban contexts. 

# Research Questions
### Our overarching research question asks: How is urban infrastructure related to crime rates and types in the city of Chicago?
Through this primary question, we seek to understand how the physical design of Chicago and the demographics of Chicago housing units influence where, when, and how crime occurs. To come to conclusions about this topic, we will examine several more specific questions that address different aspects of the various regions in Chicago.
### Public Transit Accessibility
Are areas near public transit stops more likely to experience crimes?
### Street Design
How does sidewalk coverage correlate with crime incidents?
### Street Lighting
Are neighborhoods with better street lighting associated with lower night time crime rates?
### Household Composition
Do neighborhoods with more single-person or renter households experience different patterns of crime?
### Affordable Housing Composition
Are areas with higher concentrations of affordable housing associated with higher or lower rates specific types of crimes?

In answering these more specific questions, we aim to paint a more comprehensive picture of how urban infrastructure and housing factors interact to shape patterns of crime all across Chicago. With this information we can come to conclusions about our overarching question about the relationship between the composition of a city and crime rates and types in the city of Chicago.

# Datasets
### Chicago Crime Reports 
https://data.cityofchicago.org/stories/s/Crimes-2001-to-present-Dashboard/5cd6-ry5g

A comprehensive dataset containing all reported crime incidents in Chicago from 2001 to the present including information such as date, time, location, and type of offense.

### Chicago 311 Service Requests
https://data.cityofchicago.org/Service-Requests/311-Service-Requests/v6vf-nfxy/data_preview

A dataset containing public service requests such as streetlight outages and sidewalk complaints made through Chicago’s 311 system.

### CTA Bus Stops
https://data.cityofchicago.org/Transportation/CTA-Bus-Stops-Shapefile/pxug-u72f/about_data

A dataset of all CTA (Chicago Transit Authority) bus stop locations.

### Chicago Street Centerlines
https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/transportation-2be00

A dataset of street centerlines and road network geometry within Chicago.

### Affordable Housing 
https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/dcd/supp_info/citywide-affordable-rental-housing-aalysis.html

A dataset containing the distribution and characteristics of affordable housing across Chicago. 

### Demographic Data
https://www.housingstudies.org/data-portal/browse/?view_as=view-table

A data portal from DePaul’s Institute for Housing Studies containing a large variety of housing related demographics.
